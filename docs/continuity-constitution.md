# Elysium · The Continuity Constitution

A permanent philosophical reference for all future work on continuity, memory, persistence, and temporal atmosphere in Elysium. This document is doctrinal. It is not a feature plan. No EA ticket touching continuity, memory, resurfacing, persistence, or temporal phrasing may bypass this constitution. When this constitution and a proposal disagree, the proposal is wrong.

---

## Preamble

Elysium is not a tracker, a journal, a wellness companion, or a self-optimization tool. It is a small architectural object — a private, slow-aging room. Most software, even software that postures as calm, ultimately treats the user as a data subject: behaviors recorded, totals displayed, performance evaluated, patterns surfaced. Elysium is a refusal of that posture.

The next era of Elysium's evolution is the era of **continuity** — the property that turns a daily app into an inhabited place. This is the most fragile axis of design Elysium will undertake. Every decision in this axis is one prudent choice away from becoming exactly what Elysium exists to refuse. This constitution exists so that no future team — including a future version of the current team — can mistakenly cross the line.

What follows is binding.

## Article I — Definitions

### §1 · Core continuity philosophy

1.1 — Continuity in Elysium is **sedimentation**, not **accumulation**.
1.2 — Days do not pile up into a score. They settle into atmosphere.
1.3 — A tracker tells the user what they did. Elysium reminds the room what it has held.
1.4 — The user does not *read* their continuity. They *inhabit* it.
1.5 — Continuity is a property of the room, not a feature of the app. If a continuity proposal can be described as a "feature the user uses," it is the wrong proposal.
1.6 — The unit of continuity is not the day, the week, the streak, or the session. The unit of continuity is **the room's memory of having been entered**.
1.7 — If a continuity gesture can be turned into a verb the user does ("review," "look back," "check"), it is wrong. Continuity verbs must belong to the room: *retain, deepen, soften, settle, age, return*.

### §2 · Atmospheric continuity

2.1 — Atmospheric continuity is continuity expressed as **environmental change**, not informational content.
2.2 — It is rendered in: opacity, hue, gradient depth, separator weight, transition pacing, ambient glow, phrasing tone, qualitative period labels, single italic lines.
2.3 — It is never rendered in: counts, percentages, charts, lists, calendars-with-status, comparative phrasing, timelines, badges, totals.
2.4 — Atmospheric continuity may be **felt** when noticed and **invisible** when not. A working atmospheric gesture is one most users will never consciously articulate.
2.5 — If a screenshot taken today and a screenshot taken six months later are *visibly identical*, atmospheric continuity has failed. If the difference is *legible as a metric*, it has also failed. The signal must live between those two failures.

### §3 · Sedimentary software

3.1 — Sedimentary software changes slowly and reversibly under use, the way physical objects acquire patina, wear, and warmth.
3.2 — The defining property of sedimentary software is that **time itself becomes a felt material**.
3.3 — Sedimentary software has no "progress." It has *layers*. Layers may deepen with use, soften with absence, and recompose with the natural rhythms of life (cycles, seasons, years).
3.4 — Sedimentation must be **bounded** (capped, never unbounded growth), **reversible** (softened by absence, never permanent), and **sub-perceptual per-day** (no day-over-day visible change).
3.5 — Sedimentary effects accumulate to a perceivable difference only over **weeks at minimum, months by default, years at the ceiling**. Anything visible day-to-day is not sedimentation — it is feedback, and feedback is tracking.

### §4 · Felt not read

4.1 — Information in Elysium is **felt**, not **read**, whenever its meaning depends on something other than literal content.
4.2 — A bronze trace bloom is felt. A "+1 streak" toast is read.
4.3 — A daily italic line is felt when its tone shifts; it is read when it states a number.
4.4 — The "felt not read" test: would removing all literal text from the continuity surface still leave the gesture working? If yes, it is felt. If no — if the meaning lives in the words — at minimum the words must be qualitative, never quantitative, never comparative, and finite in vocabulary.
4.5 — Felt content is curated, not generated. A finite, hand-written vocabulary of italic lines is felt. A model-generated reflection is read — and worse, performed.
4.6 — When in doubt: **fewer words, no numbers, qualitative period names over absolute times, italic over bold, presence over absence-of-something.**

### §5 · Environmental persistence

5.1 — Environmental persistence is the deliberate, slow alteration of the *room itself* (not its contents) by the fact of the user having been in it.
5.2 — It is expressed primarily through the ambient layer: `--temple-grad`, separator weights, bronze hue, transition pacing, period gradient alphas.
5.3 — Environmental persistence operates on **the room**, never on **the user**. The app may become subtly more itself; it must not produce a portrait of the user.
5.4 — Environmental persistence is **bilateral**: it deepens with presence and softens with absence. Anything that only accumulates is a streak by another name.
5.5 — Environmental persistence must remain **sub-articulable**. If a user could accurately describe what changed and when, the effect was too loud.
5.6 — Environmental persistence is the only honest form of long-term "progress" in Elysium. There is no other.

### §6 · Reversibility

6.1 — Every continuity effect that deepens with use must soften with absence. No exceptions.
6.2 — Reversibility is not a graceful afterthought. It is the structural guarantee that continuity is not a streak.
6.3 — The asymmetry between accumulation and decay may be tuned, but decay must exist, must be felt at human timescales (weeks to months, not decades), and must never require user action to trigger.
6.4 — A continuity proposal that has no decay path is automatically rejected. Write the softening rule before writing the deepening rule.
6.5 — Reversibility applies even to stored content: chronicle entries persist, but their **resurfacing weight** must decay over time so the well does not become a museum.

## Article II — Permanence and Decay

### §7 · What continuity may persist

7.1 — **Chronicle notes** persist indefinitely. They are the most precious data in the app.
7.2 — **Mind reflections** persist indefinitely. Treat them with chronicle weight.
7.3 — **Sleep closure notes** (the optional prose attached to closing the day) persist indefinitely. They are not metadata; they are short distillations.
7.4 — **The fact of presence** (closures occurred, light was witnessed, mind was held, chronicle was written) persists indefinitely. The *fact*, not the *count*.
7.5 — **Care cycle position history** is mathematically derivable and therefore persists implicitly. It is never displayed as history; it is used as a coordinate for resurfacing.
7.6 — **Atmospheric coordinates** of writing/witnessing acts (period bucket, cycle position) may be persisted as metadata on each entry — *only* in service of resurfacing, *never* as displayable analytics.

### §8 · What continuity must fade

8.1 — **Visible weight of recent days** must fade with age. Recent vivid; old gauzy; ancient ghost. This applies to Drift, the Well, traces, and any future resurfacing surface.
8.2 — **Within-day timestamps** must fade by the next day. Tomorrow does not need to know the minute light was witnessed today; it needs only to know light was witnessed.
8.3 — **Trace effects** (EA-90 bronze bloom and successors) must fade within a single render cycle or short TTL. They are not status; they are residue.
8.4 — **Patina** (gradient depth, separator weight, bronze warmth) must soften during absence. Months of absence should return the room toward, though not all the way to, its installed baseline.
8.5 — **Resurfacing relevance** of stored entries must fade with age. A chronicle line three months old surfaces more readily than one three years old; both can surface, but density of return must thin over time so the well does not crowd.

### §9 · What continuity must NEVER become visible

9.1 — **Cumulative counts of anything.** Days kept. Nights closed. Witnesses. Sessions. Reflections written. Words written. None are ever rendered as a number, even briefly, even contextually, even as a sub-line.
9.2 — **Streaks, in any spelling.** "Three in a row." "Consecutive." "Kept-in-a-row." All forbidden. Threshold-based qualitative phrasing ("A third night, quietly") is permitted *only* if it is finite, hand-curated, contains no number, and resets silently on break.
9.3 — **Comparative phrasing.** "More than last week." "Longer than usual." "Your typical…" Forbidden.
9.4 — **Ranked anything.** Best, longest, deepest, most frequent. Forbidden.
9.5 — **Time-window aggregates.** "This week." "This month." "Past 30 days." Forbidden. Years and lifetime-of-use are permitted *only* as atmospheric coordinates, never as labels.
9.6 — **Heat-style visualizations.** Calendar-with-status, density grids, ribbons of completion dots, sparklines. Forbidden — including in "subtle" or "minimalist" disguise.
9.7 — **Pattern observations about the user.** "You often write at dusk." "You tend to witness in the morning." Even if accurate, even if quiet, even if italic. Forbidden. The room may *behave* as if it knows; it must never *say* so.

### §10 · What must NEVER be stored

10.1 — **Mood ratings or affective scores**, numeric or pictorial. A mood scored is a mood flattened.
10.2 — **Inferred sentiment** from chronicle, reflection, or sleep-note text. Sentiment analysis is the precise genre Elysium opposes.
10.3 — **Behavior derivatives.** Typical bedtime. Average session length. Preferred witnessing hour. Any value that is *about the user* rather than *by the user*.
10.4 — **Anything the user did not explicitly enter.** No passive sensing, no inferred attendance, no implicit logs of opens/closes that are then displayed.
10.5 — **Counts of any user actions intended for display.** If a count is needed internally for a derivation (e.g., trace TTLs), it may exist transiently but must never be persisted or surfaced.
10.6 — **Cross-user comparative data.** Cohort norms. Population averages. "Most users at this stage…" Forbidden in storage and in concept.

## Article III — Memory Rules

### §11 · Rules for resurfacing

11.1 — Resurfacing is **deterministic**, not ranked. Algorithms that select "the best" or "the most relevant" entry are forbidden. Selection must be by date math, cycle math, or period math — never by content scoring.
11.2 — Resurfacing presents **one entry at a time** per surface. The Well presents one. A successor cross-domain well may present at most two, on rare days, with one being the standard and the second being conspicuously rare.
11.3 — Resurfacing is **silent on absence**. If no entry meets the criteria, the surface is hidden, not filled with placeholder copy.
11.4 — Resurfacing is **non-actionable**. The resurfaced entry may not be tappable to navigate to a "view full entry" screen. It is offered, not opened. Tap targets that turn resurfacing into navigation are forbidden.
11.5 — Resurfacing may not be **searched, filtered, browsed, or paged**. There is no "show me more from this date." The well speaks; the user listens.
11.6 — Resurfacing labels (eyebrow phrasing such as "A year ago today," "From an earlier turn") must be drawn from a small, hand-curated vocabulary. No dynamic templating with numbers ("3 years ago," "47 days back"). The label is qualitative.
11.7 — Resurfacing must **respect silence as a valid state**. Many days will surface nothing. This is correct behavior, not a bug.

### §12 · Rules for temporal memory

12.1 — Memory in Elysium is keyed by **atmospheric coordinates** before chronological ones. Period bucket, cycle position, lunation, and natural anniversaries are preferred over raw date offsets.
12.2 — The only chronological intervals permitted as direct coordinates are: **today, yesterday, this week** (only inside Chronicle Drift, never elsewhere), **one year ago** (anniversary anchor), and **lifetime-of-use** (for patina). Other intervals — "30 days," "this month," "this quarter," "6 months" — are forbidden as displayed labels.
12.3 — Memory must remain **derivable**, not denormalized. Resurfacing logic should compute from existing state at render time. Pre-computed "memory caches" are forbidden — they are the seed of a memory database.
12.4 — Memory may not maintain **its own state shape**. Continuity-related metadata may be attached to existing entries (e.g., a `period` field on a chronicle entry), but a top-level `memory` or `history` key in state is forbidden.
12.5 — Memory must **degrade gracefully** on missing or sparse data. A new user with no history must see an app that is *quiet*, not an app that is *broken or empty*. Silence is the correct state for an unused room.

### §13 · Rules for cross-domain resonance

13.1 — Cross-domain continuity is **resonant**, not **correlative**. Resonance recognizes that two domain entries share an atmospheric coordinate (same period, same cycle, same anniversary). Correlation derives behavioral observations from co-occurrence. Resonance is allowed; correlation is forbidden.
13.2 — Cross-domain surfaces must never become a **unified status grid**. Domains live adjacent in Temple; that adjacency is sufficient. A view that aggregates all domains into a single dashboard, however quiet, is forbidden.
13.3 — Resonance may **weave** two domains into a single phrase ("A line was left. The light was seen.") *only* when both genuinely co-occurred and the phrase remains under a strict character ceiling. Three-domain weaves are forbidden. Composition over enumeration.
13.4 — Cross-domain resurfacing may pull from multiple writing surfaces (chronicle, reflections, sleep notes) into a single well, *only* if the user cannot tell which surface the entry came from at a glance. Ambiguity of origin is a feature; it humbles the filing-cabinet impulse.
13.5 — Cross-domain effects may not **escalate**. Touching three domains in a day must not produce a louder, larger, or more decorated daily line than touching one. Composition is qualitative; volume is forbidden.

## Article IV — Domain Constitutions

### §14 · Rules for Temple continuity

14.1 — Temple is the **room's spokesperson**, not the user's dashboard. It speaks for the room; it does not report on the user.
14.2 — The daily line may have variants, may compose two domains, may acknowledge absence and return, may shift tone by duration. It may never contain a number, a comparative, or a name of a time-window.
14.3 — The temple light gradient (`--temple-grad`) is the **primary organ of environmental persistence**. It is the only surface permitted to age. All patina effects flow through it.
14.4 — Domain card state labels remain **qualitative, single-word or short-phrase, hand-curated**. No "2d ago," no "last: Tuesday," no minute-level timestamps.
14.5 — Temple may not gain a "history," "memory," "timeline," or "archive" sub-screen. Temple is the entry to the room; not a museum within the room.
14.6 — The trace effect (EA-90 and successors) is the only permitted post-action feedback on Temple. It must remain transient (sub-3-second), single-fade, no toast, no badge.
14.7 — Temple's "ambient breath" (EA-85) may gain continuity weighting (slightly deeper during long sustained presence) but the per-day variance must remain sub-perceptual, and the effect must soften during absence.

### §15 · Rules for Chronicle continuity

15.1 — Chronicle is the **deep well**. It is the most data-rich surface in the app and the surface most at risk of becoming a database. It must remain a writing place that occasionally speaks back.
15.2 — Drift remains **finite, recent, fading by time** (not by index position). The current 12-entry cap is a default ceiling; the cap may shrink for very old data but may not grow.
15.3 — The Well surfaces **one** entry by deterministic rules (anniversary first, anniversary-window second, deep-time threshold third). A second well, when introduced, must be rare, single, and visually subordinate.
15.4 — Chronicle may **never** gain a search field, a filter, a tag system, a category system, a folder system, or a date-picker. The chronicle is unindexed by design.
15.5 — Chronicle may **never** gain a "browse all entries" view. Drift, the Well, and the writing surface are the only chronicle UIs that exist.
15.6 — Resurfacing labels in the Well must age qualitatively: "A year ago today" → "From an earlier season" → "From the beginning." Finite vocabulary, no numbers.
15.7 — Chronicle may **never** display word counts, entry counts, writing-day counts, or any quantification of the user's writing practice.
15.8 — Resurfacing must respect the user's right to write into silence. Many entries will never resurface. This is not a flaw; it is the well's discretion.

### §16 · Rules for Light continuity

16.1 — Light is **spatial memory**. Continuity in Light is expressed through the sky surface itself, not through lists or counts.
16.2 — Yesterday's witnesses may persist visually as **fading horizon marks** behind today's. The fade decays across a small finite number of days, then the marks are gone — not stored as visible history.
16.3 — The Apollo eyebrow may deepen tonally with long sustained witnessing in a consistent period ("APOLLO · FIRST LIGHT" → "APOLLO · YOUR HOUR") *only* with: a long threshold, finite vocabulary, no number, reversibility on absence. Considered borderline; deploy with restraint or not at all.
16.4 — Witnessing must remain **always available, never required, never counted, never compared**. The button must never be disabled by completion state.
16.5 — Same-period reciprocity ("Met again.") may appear as a transient line *inside the modal only*, *only* when the same period was witnessed yesterday, *only* once per witness event, never persistent.
16.6 — Light may not produce a **calendar, ribbon, or grid of witnessed days**. The sky is the entire UI of Light memory.
16.7 — Witness times must fade to opacity zero across a small number of days. There is no "all-time witness log." There is the sky today, and a soft echo of yesterday.

### §17 · Rules for Sleep continuity

17.1 — Sleep is **closure**. Continuity in Sleep is expressed through the *tone* of the closure ritual, not through bedtime statistics.
17.2 — Bedtime times are stored as raw data internally but **may never be displayed as analytics**. No "average bedtime," no "consistency," no graph, no trend.
17.3 — A previous sleep note may surface inside the Sleep modal as a **single faint italic line** ("Yesterday: '…'") — once, at modal open, when one exists and is short enough. Never longer than one short line. Never multiple.
17.4 — The Hypnos eyebrow may shift tonally at long thresholds ("HYPNOS · KEPT," "HYPNOS · A LONG QUIET") with finite curated vocabulary, no numbers, reversibility.
17.5 — The morning reciprocity (EA-95) and its successors must remain **time-of-day-windowed and bilateral**: present after a closure, absent if no closure occurred, with no accumulated counter.
17.6 — Sleep notes may be drawn into the Chronicle Well rotation at deep-time thresholds, blurring the line between writing surfaces *in service of ambiguity*, never in service of search or browsing.
17.7 — Sleep may **never** become a "sleep tracker." No bed-time goals, no schedule analysis, no "you slept X hours" (the app does not even know wake time and must not be tempted to ask).

### §18 · Rules for Mind continuity

18.1 — Mind reflections are **inscriptions on the room**, not entries in a log. They are written carefully and deserve to live somewhere visible without becoming a list.
18.2 — The single most recent reflection may surface as a **single italic sub-line on the Temple Mind card** when it exists. The card may show only the most recent; older reflections do not appear here.
18.3 — Mind may **never** produce: total minutes held, average session length, "you've meditated X days this month," practice level, badges, milestones, or any session-count UI.
18.4 — The 60-second floor (EA-99) is structural and may not be replaced with a "session length goal." It is a *threshold of presence*, not a *goal of duration*.
18.5 — Reflections may enter the Chronicle Well rotation at deep-time thresholds, becoming part of the cross-domain well. Origin may be ambiguous to the user; this is intentional.
18.6 — The Hold's breath animation may, at extreme thresholds and with rigorous capping, adopt a slightly personalized rhythm — but this is considered borderline behavioral derivation and should be approached with extreme caution. **Default position: do not build.**
18.7 — Sessions never compete with each other. No "longest hold." No "deepest session." Each hold is its own complete thing.

### §19 · Rules for future domains

19.1 — Body and Water are dormant by design. They may be activated only when their absence becomes a genuine ache in the product, never as the next available feature box to tick.
19.2 — Any new domain must inherit the full continuity constitution before its first ticket. No domain is grandfathered or exempted.
19.3 — A new domain may not be built as a "tracking domain" in any framing. If the domain's primary primitive is a measurement, the domain is wrong-shaped for Elysium.
19.4 — Each new domain must declare, before implementation: what persists, what fades, what may never be stored, what its trace looks like, and how it acknowledges absence.
19.5 — A new domain may not introduce a new top-level continuity surface (history, archive, log). It must thread its continuity through the existing primitives: Temple greeting, Temple trace, Chronicle Well rotation, and its own domain-specific atmospheric organ.
19.6 — A new domain that cannot meet these constraints is not ready to be built — even if the product feels incomplete without it.

### §19A · The Care Practical Exception

19A.1 — Care is Elysium's practical anchor. It predates the app and defines the app's reason for existing. Unlike the atmospheric ritual domains, Care involves active skincare ingredients — niacinamide, salicylic acid — governed by a 3-day ingredient protocol. That protocol has practical continuity requirements that atmospheric design alone cannot satisfy. The cycle position depends on whether last night's care was kept; the user deserves to know this.

19A.2 — The Care Practical Exception permits, **only for Care**, limited practical completion history that would otherwise be forbidden under §9.6 and §20.3. Specifically allowed for Care:
- (a) Daily kept/not-kept status for skincare nights, stored in `loggedDays`
- (b) Per-day partial completion state derived from `state.checks`
- (c) A calendar view of Care history — showing kept, partial, and quiet nights
- (d) Constellation lines connecting adjacent kept nights — aesthetic geometry, no labels, no density scores
- (e) The 3-day cycle position display: current cycle day, tonight's protocol label ("Night I · Niacinamide", "Night II · Salicylic", "Rest night"), and the cycle memory that derives it
- (f) A per-day record surface showing: date, cycle protocol for that night, and an optional chronicle excerpt — read-only, non-browsable, reached by tapping a calendar cell
- (g) A "correct record" facility allowing a user to retroactively mark or clear a night as kept — because the app cannot observe real-world behavior, and cycle accuracy serves a practical skincare purpose
- (h) A passive milestone stage label ("foundation", "acne control", "marks and texture", "maintenance") — a qualitative life-stage phrase derived from days elapsed, not a score, not a percentage, not a daily-changing indicator

19A.3 — The rationale for each permitted element: the calendar and `loggedDays` history exist so users can verify cycle position and identify gaps before applying active ingredients — this is practical safety, not behavioral analytics. The "correct record" facility preserves cycle accuracy when the app missed a real-world action. The constellation lines are aesthetic; they carry no information content beyond the presence of nearby kept nights. The stage label is qualitative and tracks months-long life stages, not daily performance.

19A.4 — **The following remain forbidden, even for Care:**
- Streak counts, streak labels, or streak-emphasizing copy of any kind
- Percentages, ratios, or adherence scores ("4 of 7 nights", "80% kept")
- Cumulative day counts displayed to the user ("142 days logged")
- Weekly or monthly summaries or recaps
- Comparative phrasing ("more nights than last week", "your best run")
- Pattern observations about Care behavior ("you tend to skip Sundays")
- Achievement badges, milestone celebrations, or positive-reinforcement copy beyond the passive stage label
- Any expansion of the Care completion grid to include non-Care data

19A.5 — **This exception does not extend to any other domain.** The following are explicitly excluded from Care Practical Exception coverage:
- **Water** — holdings tracked internally for boolean presence only; no calendar, no completion grid, no held-count display
- **Body** — arrivals tracked internally for boolean presence only; no calendar, no completion grid, no arrival-count display
- **Mind** — arrivals and reflections tracked for atmospheric continuity; no session analytics, no calendar, no reflection-frequency display
- **Sleep** — closures tracked for morning reciprocity and Well resurfacing; no calendar, no closure-rate display, no consistency analytics
- **Light** — witnesses tracked per-day for horizon marks; no calendar, no witness grid, no frequency display
- **Chronicle** — notes stored indefinitely; no writing-frequency calendar, no days-written grid, no word counts
- **Temple** — atmosphere and domain state labels only; no aggregate status grid, no cross-domain completion dashboard
- **Day Thread** — qualitative presence traces only; no completion list, no ritual grid
- **Closing Summary** — qualitative open-items list only; no completion rates, no performance framing
- **Recent Presence Atmosphere** — gradient lift and daily-line variants only; no week-view calendar, no presence grid

19A.6 — This exception is narrow by doctrine. Future proposals that appeal to §19A to authorize new Care tracking surfaces bear the burden of proof: they must demonstrate a skincare-specific practical need that cannot be met without a completion history surface. Habit formation, engagement, or retention goals are not sufficient justification. When in doubt, the exception does not extend.

19A.7 — If the Progress/Stars calendar ever begins surfacing non-Care data — Water presence, Body arrivals, Mind sessions, Sleep closures, Light witnesses — the exception has been violated regardless of how the feature is framed. The calendar grid and `loggedDays` mechanism are Care-only infrastructure.

## Article V — Prohibitions

### §20 · Continuity anti-patterns

The following are categorically forbidden. This list is non-exhaustive but normative — any feature in the family of these patterns is forbidden by analogy.

20.1 — Streaks in any spelling.
20.2 — Cumulative counters (days, nights, sessions, words, witnesses, anything).
20.3 — Heatmaps, density grids, calendar-with-status visualizations.
20.4 — Weekly recaps, monthly summaries, end-of-year reviews.
20.5 — Trend lines, sparklines, bar charts, any quantitative graph.
20.6 — Comparative phrasing ("more than usual," "longest in weeks").
20.7 — Pattern observations ("you often…", "you tend to…").
20.8 — Recommendation systems ("today try…", "consider…").
20.9 — Re-engagement notifications ("we miss you," "come back").
20.10 — AI-generated summaries, reflections, or insights of any kind.
20.11 — Sentiment/mood inference from user text.
20.12 — Mood ratings, numeric or pictorial.
20.13 — Achievement badges, milestones, levels, ranks.
20.14 — Social/inter-user features of any kind, including stub mentions of "shared rituals."
20.15 — A "memory" tab, "history" view, "archive" screen, or "timeline."
20.16 — Search, filter, tag, or category systems over user writing.
20.17 — Browse-all-entries views for any writing surface.
20.18 — Confetti, celebrations, sound effects, haptics tied to "achievement."
20.19 — Tooltips explaining what a continuity feature means.
20.20 — Settings to "enable" or "disable" continuity features (it must be quiet enough that no setting is needed).

### §21 · Forbidden directions

The following directions are forbidden as *categories* of work. No proposal in these categories may be accepted regardless of how it is framed.

21.1 — Building any feature whose value scales with **volume of data**. Anything that "gets more interesting the more you use it" in a quantitative sense is a tracker.
21.2 — Building any **layer of abstraction over user writing** (tagging, categorization, sentiment, summarization, clustering).
21.3 — Building any **explicit memory surface** (a tab, a screen, a section dedicated to remembering).
21.4 — Building any feature that compares the user to **themselves over time periods**.
21.5 — Building any feature that compares the user to **other users**.
21.6 — Building any **AI-mediated layer** between the user and their writing.
21.7 — Building any **passive sensing** (location, motion, biometrics) that produces displayable data.
21.8 — Building any **integration with external trackers** (HealthKit, Apple Health, Fitbit, Oura, etc.). Elysium is sovereign and self-contained.
21.9 — Building any **export of historical data as a report or formatted view**. Plain JSON export for portability is permitted; rendered "your year in Elysium" PDFs are forbidden.
21.10 — Building any **algorithmic ranking** of resurfaced content.

### §22 · Philosophical red lines

These are the deepest commitments. Crossing them is not a course-correction — it is a different product.

22.1 — Elysium does not **measure**. It may *witness*; it may not *quantify*.
22.2 — Elysium does not **observe the user**. It may *acknowledge presence*; it may not *describe behavior*.
22.3 — Elysium does not **perform** on the user's behalf. There is no synthesis, no recap, no auto-generated summary, no AI voice that is not the user's.
22.4 — Elysium does not **reward**. There is no celebration of milestones, no positive reinforcement, no congratulation. The room neither praises nor blames.
22.5 — Elysium does not **suggest**. There is no recommendation, no nudge, no "you might like." The user leads; the room follows.
22.6 — Elysium does not **pursue the user**. Notifications are cues for *present* moments (morning cue, night cue), never re-engagement pulls.
22.7 — Elysium does not **monetize attention**. There is no engagement metric to optimize, no DAU/WAU framing internally, no growth lever to pull. If a design decision is made to "increase usage," it is the wrong decision.
22.8 — Elysium does not **scale by becoming louder**. It scales by becoming deeper.
22.9 — Elysium does not **become more like the user over time**. It becomes more like *itself* — a more settled, more characterful version of the same room. Personalization is forbidden; patina is permitted.
22.10 — Elysium does not **forget the user violently**. Account deletion erases data; absence merely softens patina. Departure is reversible; deletion is final.

## Article VI — Discriminations

### §23 · The difference between continuity and tracking

| Continuity (permitted) | Tracking (forbidden) |
|---|---|
| The room remembers being entered. | The app records how often you entered. |
| Yesterday's light leaves a faint mark in the sky. | "You witnessed light 5 of the last 7 days." |
| A chronicle line from a year ago surfaces today. | "View all chronicle entries by date." |
| The Sleep modal greets you with last night's note. | "Your average bedtime this week was 22:47." |
| The Hypnos eyebrow shifts tonally at long thresholds. | "You have a 14-day closure streak." |
| The temple light gradient deepens with months of use. | "You have used Elysium 142 days." |
| The daily line acknowledges absence and return. | "Welcome back! You've been gone 8 days." |

Test: if the effect can be reduced to a number without losing its meaning, it is tracking. If the effect's meaning lives in its environmental presence, it is continuity.

### §24 · The difference between atmosphere and analytics

24.1 — Atmosphere is **render-time and ambient**. It changes the room. Analytics is **read-time and informational**. It changes what the user knows about themselves.
24.2 — Atmosphere has no source view. There is nothing to drill into. Analytics has a drill-down by definition.
24.3 — Atmosphere can be ignored without loss of meaning. Analytics demands interpretation.
24.4 — Atmosphere requires no glossary. Analytics requires labels, axes, legends, and explanations.
24.5 — Atmosphere is **categorically uninteresting to a productivity-app user**. Analytics is *exactly* what they came for. If a productivity-app user would find a feature satisfying, the feature is probably analytics.

### §25 · The difference between ritual and productivity

25.1 — A ritual is **completed by being done**. A productivity task is **completed by being recorded**. Elysium's primitives are rituals; the record is a residue, not the point.
25.2 — A ritual produces **presence**. A productivity task produces **output**. Continuity in Elysium must reinforce presence, not output.
25.3 — A ritual may be *kept* or *not kept* — it cannot be *failed*. Productivity tasks fail when missed; rituals merely pass.
25.4 — A ritual's value does not compound by repetition into a measurable trajectory. Its value is felt in the doing, in the room's recognition, in the soft persistence of *having been done*.
25.5 — Continuity built on a productivity frame ("you completed Care 4 of 7 nights") corrupts ritual into task. Continuity built on a ritual frame ("the night has been kept") preserves the form.
25.6 — When a designer instinctively reaches for "completion percentage" — that is the precise moment productivity is invading. Stop.

## Article VII — The Long View

### §26 · How Elysium should feel after years of use

26.1 — Opening Elysium after three years should feel like **opening a room you have lived in**, not opening a file cabinet that has filled up.
26.2 — The user should not be able to recite their statistics, because there are none. They should be able to recall the room — its tone, its quiet, its presences — the way one recalls a study or a kitchen.
26.3 — The temple light should be **slightly richer** than on day one — perceptible to a returning user if they were to compare side-by-side, but not articulable in daily use.
26.4 — Separator hairlines should be **slightly more present**. The architecture of the room should be **slightly more legible**. The bronze should be **slightly more itself**.
26.5 — Chronicle should feel **deep** — not in the sense of "look at how much I've written," but in the sense of the well returning unexpected lines the user had forgotten writing.
26.6 — The Sleep modal should occasionally surface a note from a previous self that startles in its specificity. Not a recap. A single line from a Tuesday three years ago.
26.7 — Mind reflections should accumulate as inscriptions, not as a meditation log. A returning user should encounter their old reflections rarely, and when they do, the encounter should feel *given*, not *queried*.
26.8 — Light should feel like a sky the user *knows* — its color shifts, its golden-hour familiarity, the faint ghosts of yesterdays in its horizon — without ever telling the user "you've witnessed N times."
26.9 — Cross-domain weaves should feel like the room **occasionally finishing the user's sentence** — a chronicle from a salicylic night returning on the next salicylic night, paired (rarely) with a sleep note from the same period. The room speaks the user's grammar.
26.10 — A long absence followed by return should be met not with welcome-back fanfare, not with re-engagement guilt, but with **the single quietest possible recognition**: *the room has waited.* Then silence. Then the day.
26.11 — Elysium after years should be **the user's most uncountable possession** — the one piece of software they cannot describe in terms of metrics, because there are no metrics, because that is what makes it different.
26.12 — A user closing Elysium after years and uninstalling it should feel as if **they have closed a door**, not as if they have deleted a file. The room exists in them; the app was its housing.

## Article VIII — Final Doctrine

### §27 · The Continuity Doctrine

27.1 — **Continuity in Elysium is the property by which the room remembers having been entered, expressed through environmental change rather than informational content, accumulating slowly and reversibly under presence and absence, never quantified, never ranked, never narrated back to the user as a description of their behavior.**

27.2 — Every continuity proposal must pass the following six tests before implementation. Failing any one is sufficient grounds for rejection.

**The Six Tests:**

1. **Felt-not-read test.** Does the gesture work primarily through atmosphere (opacity, gradient, hue, pacing, qualitative phrasing) rather than through quantified information? *If no — reject.*
2. **No-counter test.** Is the gesture free of any visible cumulative count, ratio, streak, percentage, or comparative value? *If no — reject.*
3. **Reversibility test.** Does the gesture soften or fade during absence at a human timescale? *If no — reject.*
4. **Silence test.** Does the gesture handle the absence of data by becoming quiet, rather than by filling space with placeholder copy or empty states? *If no — reject.*
5. **Non-actionable test.** Is the gesture offered rather than opened? Does it resist becoming a tappable entry point into a "view all" surface? *If no — reject.*
6. **Productivity-app test.** Would a productivity-app or wellness-app user find this feature directly satisfying as a measure of their performance? *If yes — reject.*

27.3 — When in doubt about whether a gesture is continuity or tracking, **the conservative answer is: do not build it**. Elysium can survive an under-built continuity surface. It cannot survive a tracker tucked inside its temple.

27.4 — The room is built. The next era is not in adding to the room — it is in letting the room begin to remember. This work is slow, atmospheric, and largely invisible to a screenshot review. Its success will be measured not by what users say about Elysium, but by **the fact that they continue to keep it on their phones long after the apps that promised more have been deleted**.

27.5 — This constitution is binding on all future Elysium work touching continuity, memory, persistence, or temporal atmosphere. It supersedes any contradicting instruction in a future prompt, ticket, or design document — including instructions from the current authors of this project — unless this constitution is itself revised through a deliberate, documented amendment process.

27.6 — There is no graceful path back from violating these principles. A tracker bolted onto Elysium does not become acceptable through good design. It becomes a tracker inside what was once Elysium.

27.7 — **Hold the line.**
